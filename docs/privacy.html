Vox

Privacy policy

Last updated: 30 September 2026

Vox is a free, open-source Windows and Android app for voice dictation and meeting notes. It has no analytics, no accounts and no server of its own. The developer does not receive, collect or store any of your data. Your audio and text go only to servers you choose, as described below.

Where your data goes

Vox has three kinds of server, and you pick every one of them in Settings:

  • Your speech server turns audio into text. By default this is Groq (groq.com) with your own API key.
  • Your cleanup server tidies the text. By default this is also Groq.
  • Your relay is an optional small server that you run yourself. It is off by default.

You can pick another provider (for example OpenAI, OpenRouter, Together or Mistral) or enter the address of your own OpenAI-compatible server (for example Whisper on your own computer, over your local network or Tailscale). Plain http is only accepted for this device, your local network and Tailscale addresses. Any other server must use https. Each server receives only its own key. What each server provider does with data is covered by that provider's own privacy policy and terms.

On Windows, a long recording is sent to the speech server in pieces while you are still speaking (switch stream_stt, on by default). Short recordings are sent as one piece. On Android a recording is sent as one piece after you stop.

Your relay (optional)

A relay is a small program (relay/relay.py) that you run on your own machine, for example a Raspberry Pi on your Tailscale network. Vox only talks to a relay if you turn one of these switches on, or press Test next to the relay address or Refresh on the Devices card, and you have entered its address and token. Sync, Test and Devices requests carry the name of your device. The Devices card asks the relay which devices have used it and shows each one's name, whether it is this device and when it was last seen.

Turning this off on a device removes that device's keys from the relay once, at its next sync. Any other device that still shares keys will put them back, so turn it off on every device.

The relay is yours, so what it stores is up to you. It keeps synced notes and the profile in a database file, and the AI server keys you give it in plain text in relay.json (readable only by its owner on Linux and macOS). Anyone who can read those files, or who has your relay token and can reach the relay, can read your notes and profile, and any API keys you chose to sync. Keep the relay on your own private network.

Data stored on your devices

On Windows your settings, dictation history, dictionary, meeting transcripts and notes are stored on your PC, in %APPDATA%\Vox and in Documents\Vox Notes. API keys and the relay token are protected by your Windows login. Voice notes are in notes.db in the same folder. They are not encrypted. In Settings you can turn dictation history off, and then nothing you dictate is saved to history. By default Vox puts your earlier copied text back after pasting a dictation (a copied image or file is not restored). The Keep dictation on the clipboard setting (keep_clipboard) is off by default.

On Android, settings, voice notes and history are in the app's private storage. The API key, the relay token and the history are not encrypted on the phone. They are removed when you uninstall the app. The app lists the apps installed on the phone so the Styles page can offer them; this list never leaves the phone.

Uninstalling Vox and deleting the folders above removes the data on that device. It does not remove copies on a relay or on the servers of your providers.

Google Calendar (optional)

If you choose Connect Google Calendar, Vox requests read-only access to your calendar events (scope calendar.events.readonly) and your email address. Vox reads event titles, start and end times, attendee names and email addresses, the organizer, and video-call links, for the next 7 days. Vox uses this only to:

  • show your upcoming meetings in the app,
  • remind you (or start notes, if you turn that on) when a meeting begins,
  • title your notes and label who said what.

Calendar data is stored only on your PC. For meetings you choose to record, the meeting title, attendee names and organizer, and your own name from Settings, are sent to your servers as described in the table above. Vox does not modify your calendar, does not share Google user data with anyone else, does not use it for advertising, and does not use it to train AI models.

Vox's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

To disconnect, click Disconnect in Vox > Notes > Calendar, or remove Vox at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

Recording other people

Meeting notes record what others say. Tell participants before you record. Recording laws differ by place.
