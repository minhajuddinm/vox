Privacy policy

Last updated: 27 September 2026

Vox is a free, open-source Windows and Android app for voice dictation and meeting notes. It has no servers and no accounts of its own. The developer does not receive, collect or store any of your data.

Data stored on your PC

On Windows your API key is stored protected by your Windows login. In Settings you can turn dictation history off, and then nothing you dictate is saved. On Android the app lists the apps installed on the phone so the Styles page can offer them; this list never leaves the phone.

Your settings, API keys, the "About you" text, dictation history, dictionary, meeting transcripts and notes are stored only on your computer, in %APPDATA%\Vox and in Documents\Vox Notes. Uninstalling Vox and deleting these folders removes them. On Android, settings, key and history are stored in the app's private storage and are removed when you uninstall the app.

Your provider: Groq by default, or a server of your choice

To turn speech into text and to clean up text and write meeting notes, Vox sends your recorded audio and the resulting text to the server you set in Settings. By default that is Groq (groq.com) using your own Groq API key. You can pick another provider (for example OpenAI, OpenRouter, Together or Mistral) or enter the address of your own OpenAI-compatible server (for example Whisper on your own computer, reached over your local network or Tailscale). Speech and cleanup can use different servers; each server receives only its own key, your audio goes only to the speech server, and the text goes only to the cleanup server. Plain http is only accepted for this device, your local network and Tailscale addresses; any other server must use https. Along with the text, Vox tells the cleanup model the name of the app you are typing into so it can match the tone, and the text you wrote under About you (if any) so it can match your names, projects and style. When you take meeting notes, the meeting title and attendee names are included so that names are spelled and labelled correctly. When you start dictating, Vox also opens a connection to each server with a small request for its model list (your key, no audio) so that the upload is faster. The server's handling of this data is covered by that provider's own privacy policy and terms.

Google Calendar (optional)

If you choose Connect Google Calendar, Vox requests read-only access to your calendar events (scope calendar.events.readonly) and your email address. Vox reads event titles, start and end times, attendee names and email addresses, the organizer, and video-call links, for the next 7 days. Vox uses this only to:

  • show your upcoming meetings in the app,
  • remind you (or start notes, if you turn that on) when a meeting begins,
  • title your notes and label who said what.

Calendar data is stored only on your PC. Meeting titles and attendee names are sent to your server as described above, only for meetings you choose to record. Vox does not modify your calendar, does not share Google user data with anyone else, does not use it for advertising, and does not use it to train AI models.

Vox's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

To disconnect, click Disconnect in Vox > Notes > Calendar, or remove Vox at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

Recording other people

Meeting notes record what others say. Tell participants before you record. Recording laws differ by place.
